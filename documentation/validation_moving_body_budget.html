Validation: Moving-Body Mass Budget and Galilean Twin

Moving immersed boundary: gas mass created at the surface, and the heat and force of a body against its Galilean twin

Status: partially validated. Code: master at 0cdfdf02a. Example: examples/2D_ibm_moving_mass_budget. Last run: 2026-11-22 (mass runs); 2026-11-24 (twin runs, with weno_avg = T, which weno_Re_flux now requires).

Purpose

A ghost-cell immersed boundary is not conservative at a moving wall. Gas flows through the faces between the fluid and the body into ghost cells whose values are then overwritten, and cells change side as the body moves, so the gas total drifts with the distance the body travels. This measures that drift, checks the opt-in correction ib_mass_relax (the solver default for a moving body, 30), and compares the conducted heat and the force of a translating body with the same body held in the same relative flow. Both matter for particle-resolved runs, where a particle's drag and heating are read from these quantities.

Reference data

  • Exact: the gas mass of a closed periodic box with an inert body is constant.
  • Exact: a body moving through gas at rest and the same body held in gas moving at the opposite velocity feel the same relative flow, so their conducted heat and force agree (ratio 1). The twin is a self-consistency check, not a comparison with an external value.
  • Qualitative background on the force noise and the mass error of fresh and dead cells: Lee et al. [44] and Seo and Mittal [81]. Neither is used as a number here.

Quantities

  • Gas mass M: \(\sum\rho\,dV\) over the cells whose centre is outside the body at its prescribed position (periodic wrap), from the saves. Steady rate: the least-squares slope of 100 (M - M_0)/M_0 against the distance travelled after the first diameter (the impulsive start is excluded), in % of the gas per diameter.
  • Conducted heat Q: \(k\,R\!\int\partial T/\partial n\,d\theta\) at the wall, with \(\partial T/\partial n\) from a cubic fit of T along 720 rays at 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 cell widths (bilinear samples). The same estimator is used for both twins, so only the ratio is meaningful. Means over the last 3 D travelled.
  • Force: \(F_x\) of D/ib_forces.dat (ib_force_wrt, one row per step), means over the same window; the RMS of the difference of the two histories (the fixed history interpolated to the moving times, mean removed) is given in percent of the mean fixed force.
  • Script: examples/2D_ibm_moving_mass_budget/check_budget.py.

Assessment

  • A moving no-slip circle creates gas mass at 0.013-0.05 % of the gas per diameter travelled (inverse-distance image points), falling at about first order with refinement. Over the 6 D of the runs this is 0.13-0.36 %, and it would grow with the distance a particle travels.
  • ib_mass_relax = 30 holds the total to 0.016 % or less over 6 D at every grid and the steady rate to 0.002 %/D, with the start-up offset bounded.
  • With the correction, the conducted heat of a wall at 0.9 of the gas temperature agrees with its twin to 0.9 % at 16 and 32 cells/D; the correction moves the ratio from 0.94 and 0.97 and does not change the force (2 % low at 16 cells/D, 1.6 % at 32).
  • A cold wall (0.2 of the gas temperature) is not reproduced: the heat is 18 % low at 16 cells/D and 7 % low at 32 with the correction, and the force of the moving body is not close to its twin's.
  • Status is partially validated: the mass budget and the warm-wall twin close; the cold-wall twin and the force do not.

Known limitations

  • Cold moving wall. The conducted heat is low by 7-18 % at 16-32 cells/D even with the correction, converging about first order. The cause is a ghost-state error at the advancing nose that the correction does not remove (found in a separate Python prototype; not re-verified in MFC). The solver documentation lists measured twin ratios for the same case.
  • Force of a moving body. ib_forces.dat is the lab-frame face momentum flux (ib_face_ft); it has no \(-V f_m\) term and the cells a body covers or uncovers book no momentum. The twin ratio is 0.98 for a wall near the gas temperature and not meaningful for the cold wall (1.40 and 1.92 at 16 cells/D, 0.78 at 32, RMS difference 7-118 % of the fixed force). The force noise of a moving body (the cell-crossing frequency) is visible in the RMS column and was not separated here. Not changed in this example.
  • Drift with the box: 2D, one body, Mach 0.3, inviscid; 3D bodies cross cells in three directions and a particle array was not run here (the fixed-body array is covered by ib_flux_wall, see the 2D_ibm_wall_mass_budget example).
  • The reacting moving body (gasification plus swept cells) is covered by the suite's Moving Heat Output tests (regression only), not by a validation case.
  • The conducted-heat estimator is a cubic fit; its absolute value is not an independent measurement.

Reproduce

./mfc.sh run examples/2D_ibm_moving_mass_budget/case.py -t pre_process simulation -n 1 --gpu -- \
--cells_per_D 24 --mach 0.3 --box 5 --travel 6 --nsave 24 --relax 0 --wall_order 1
python3 examples/2D_ibm_moving_mass_budget/check_budget.py mass <run directory>

Twin: run the same case with --cells_per_D 16 --mach 0.3 --box 8 --Re 40 --Twall 0.2 --ip_interp 2 --travel 6 --nsave 12 --stride 1 once with --relax 0, once with --relax 30, and once with --frame fixed, then check_budget.py twin --moving <moving run> --fixed <fixed run>. A build without MPI adds --serial_io. The suite runs the CI-sized case as 2D -> IBM -> Moving Circle -> Mass Budget and ... -> Cold Wall.
