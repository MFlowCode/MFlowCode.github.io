Validation: Shock Tubes and the Shu-Osher Problem

1D Riemann problems against the exact solution, and the Shu-Osher problem

Status: validated (Sod, Lax, 123 with WENO, TENO and MUSCL; Shu-Osher against a converged reference); partially validated for IGR (Sod and Lax only). Code: master at 5838b641c. Examples: examples/1D_sod_convergence, examples/1D_sodshocktube, examples/1D_laxshocktube, examples/1D_shuosher_*. Last run: 2026-11-25.

Purpose

Shock tubes are the basic check of a shock-capturing scheme: the exact solution of the Riemann problem is known, so the error of the shock, contact and rarefaction can be measured, not only seen. This checks the single-fluid ideal-gas path (model_eqns = 2, one fluid) with each reconstruction and Riemann solver against the exact solution, and the shock-entropy-wave interaction of Shu and Osher against a converged reference.

The order of the L1 error of a solution with discontinuities is at most one: the shock and the contact are each smeared over a few cells. The 123 problem converges more slowly (0.5 to 0.65): its error sits at the centre, where the two rarefactions leave a near-vacuum ( \(\rho\) = 0.022, p = 0.0019) and the density shows the usual dip of shock-capturing schemes there; its velocity error is the largest of the three (L1 4.0e-3 at 2048 cells against 3.0e-4 for Sod).

CPU and GPU

On an A100 (NVHPC 25.11, OpenACC, parallel_io = T) the L1 errors of Sod, Lax and 123 at 1024 cells, of IGR Sod at 1024, and of both examples above agree with the GNU CPU values to all printed digits; the final densities differ by at most 1.4e-9 of the maximum (4e-10 for Sod and Lax, 1e-12 and below for the examples and IGR).

WENO5-Z, grid sweep: 7.19e-1 (143 cells), 3.47e-1 (286), 9.69e-2 (572), 5.92e-2 (1144); the error drops quickly once the post-shock waves are resolved (about 500 cells) and then at order 0.7, where the shock and the remaining wave error compete. The reference itself: WENO5-Z at 2288, 4004 and 8008 cells differs from it by 3.83e-2, 1.70e-2 and 6.23e-3 (order 1.45), so its own error is about 2.5e-3, under 4 % of the errors at 1001 cells.

At 572 and 1144 cells WENO5-Z puts the steepest density jump (the shock, x = 7.39) in the same cell as the reference. WENO5-Z is the most accurate at 1001 cells; TENO7 with teno_CT = 1e-9 is the least accurate and the least robust to round-off: the CPU and the A100 final densities differ by up to 0.10 (in the cells next to the shock and in the waves; 5e-4 after 200 steps), and the L1 error is 1.23e-1 on the CPU and 1.29e-1 on the A100. TENO5 behaves the same way at a smaller scale (densities differ by 1.1e-2; L1 8.871e-2 and 8.864e-2). WENO5-JS and WENO5-Z agree between the two to 5e-12, and the 16016-cell reference to 3e-9.

Assessment

  • The exact-solution errors of all three Riemann problems decrease at the order expected of discontinuous solutions, for every reconstruction and Riemann solver, and are the same on the CPU and the A100.
  • The ordering of the schemes is as expected: first-order WENO1 is 13 times less accurate than WENO5 at 2048 cells, MUSCL-minmod 2.7 times, and HLLC is the most accurate of the three Riemann solvers.
  • The Shu-Osher examples agree with a converged reference to 6.5e-2 to 1.2e-1 (L1 over a domain of length 10) at their default 1001 cells.

Known limitations

  • IGR has no 1D path (#2463): a 1D case with igr = T passes validation and segfaults. The IGR rows use a 2D strip of 8 cells in y (periodic) with elliptic_smoothing (10 iterations); without the smoothing the Sod strip gives NaN within 10 steps. With alf_factor = 10 its error is 6 to 7 times that of WENO5-M (the regularization widens the shock over several cells), and it converges at the same order. The 123 problem fails with IGR (NaN, also with igr_pres_lim).
  • Shu-Osher examples differ from the textbook problem. 1D_shuosher_wenojs5, -wenom5, -wenoz5, -teno5 and -teno7 put the shock at x = 1 on [0, 10] and use \(\sin 5x\) (the textbook problem is on [-5, 5] with the shock at x = -4, which shifts the phase of the wave by 0.13 rad), and round the left state to three decimals. 1D_shuosher_old and 1D_shuosher_analytical use the textbook domain but a left density of 3.957143 instead of 3.857143. The reference above is for the shipped problem, so the comparison is consistent; it is not a comparison with published figures.
  • TENO5 and TENO7 on the Shu-Osher problem are sensitive to round-off at the shock (the CPU and A100 runs differ by 1e-2 to 1e-1 in density near the shock, see above), presumably through the sharp stencil cut-off (teno_CT); not investigated further.
  • Only ideal gas and one fluid are checked here; multi-fluid shock tubes and stiffened gases are not.

Reproduce

./mfc.sh run examples/1D_sod_convergence/case.py -t pre_process simulation -n 1 -- -N 512 --problem lax
python3 examples/1D_sod_convergence/check_riemann.py <run 128> <run 256> ... <run 2048>
./mfc.sh run examples/1D_shuosher_wenoz5/case.py -t pre_process simulation -n 1 -- -N 16016
python3 examples/1D_shuosher_wenoz5/check_shuosher.py <reference run> <run> [<run> ...]

Each run writes next to its case.py, so copy the example directory per run. Other options of 1D_sod_convergence/case.py: --order, --teno, --muscl --muscl-lim, --riemann hll|hllc|lax_friedrichs, --igr 3|5. The 2048-cell Sod run takes about 10 s on one CPU core; the 16016-cell Shu-Osher reference about 9 minutes. The suite already runs the L1 self-convergence of 1D_sod_convergence for ten reconstructions (Convergence -> Sod) and the shipped examples at reduced size; no test is added here.
