Validation: Solid-Wall Thermal Models

Lumped cooling of a cylinder, and a cold wall in hot gas

Status: partially validated. Code: master at 0cdfdf02a. Examples: examples/2D_ibm_cylinder_drag_nu (lumped cooling, --thermal_bc 3) and examples/2D_ibm_cold_wall_transient. Last run: 2026-11-24 (cause of the lumped gap); 2026-11-23 (the rest).

Purpose

A particle heats or cools while it sits in the gas, and the heat it absorbs in the first microseconds sets the ignition delay of a reacting particle. Two wall models carry this in an immersed boundary: the lumped solid temperature (thermal_bc = 3, one temperature per body advanced by the heat the faces carry) and the sub-grid conduction column (patch_ib(1)%wall_column) that carries the flux of a wall layer thinner than a cell. This checks the first against the decay time its own fixed-wall Nusselt number implies, and the second against a body-fitted 1D solution.

Reference data

  • Lumped model. A body of mass per area \(\rho_s c_s D/4\) (2D cylinder) in a stream of heat-transfer coefficient \(h = \mathrm{Nu}\,k_f/D\) relaxes to the adiabatic-wall temperature with \(\tau = \rho_s c_s D^2/(4\,\mathrm{Nu}\,k_f)\). Nu and \(T_{aw}\) come from two fixed-wall thermal_bc = 1 runs of the same case and grid (wall at 1.0 and 1.1 times the free-stream temperature): MFC's own fixed-wall result, so this tests the lumped update against the face heat it is fed, not the Nusselt number against the literature (for that see Validation: Circular Cylinder Drag and Nusselt Number).
  • Cold wall. examples/2D_ibm_cold_wall_transient/ref1d.py solves conduction into the cylinder's surrounding gas at uniform pressure in the mass coordinate \(\psi = \int \rho r\,dr\) (the wall at \(\psi = 0\), no gas crosses it), \(c_p\,\partial T/\partial t = \partial_\psi(\rho r^2 \lambda\,\partial T/\partial\psi)\) with \(r^2 = R^2 + 2\int d\psi/\rho\), by finite volumes on a grid stretched from the wall and BDF in time, with the Cantera \(\lambda(T)\) and \(c_p(T)\) of the same air; the heat into the body \(Q(t) = \int 2\pi R\,q\,dt\) is carried as one more unknown. Q changes by under 2e-4 between 800 and 1600 cells and between outer radii of 8 R and 16 R, and the solver shares no code with MFC. The initial gas profile is MFC's own, \(T = T_\infty + (T_w - T_\infty)(R/r)^{1/2}\mathrm{erfc}((r-R)/\delta)\).

Quantities

  • Lumped. \(T_w(t)\) from column 6 of D/ib_heat.dat; \(\tau\) from the formula above with the twins' Nu (heat of column 5 of the hot and cold twin, the difference removing the viscous heating) and \(k_f\) at the film temperature; the fit \(T_a' + A\exp(-t/\tau')\) over \(tU/D\) above the fit start with a free asymptote and \(\mathrm{Nu}_\mathrm{fit} = \mathrm{Nu}\,\tau/\tau'\) (check_lumped_cooling.py).
  • Cold wall. \(Q(t)\), the running sum of column 5 of D/ib_heat.dat (energy into the body per unit depth, the face heat including the enthalpy of exchanged mass), against the reference at times in units of \(R^2/\alpha_\infty\) of the hot gas (check_heat.py).

Assessment

  • The lumped cylinder decays as an exponential but 5.5 to 16 % more slowly than its fixed-wall Nusselt number implies. The gap does not depend on the grid (-8.9 % at 12 cells per D and -9.2 % at 16 for Re 40; -15.9 % and -16.1 % for Re 20) and falls as the decay time grows (-16 % at \(\tau U/D\) = 6 to -5.5 % at 24 for Re 20).
  • The gap is the transient response of the gas, not an error of the lumped model (see "Cause of the gap" below): the body's temperature change equals the heat its faces book to 1e-3, and the decay that the fixed-wall twins' own linear response predicts, with no lumped update, matches the run to 0.3 % in the decay time at both decay times run. The fixed wall's heat is still 8 % above its steady value at \(tU/D\) = 3 and 3 % at 6 after the impulsive start, and the gas next to a cooling wall holds heat from when the wall was hotter; the steady Nusselt number assumes neither. The lag time of the twins' step response, 1.07 \(D/U\), gives the first-order gap \(\tau_{bl}/\tau\) = 18 % at \(\tau U/D\) = 5.9 and 4.5 % at 23.8 (measured 15.5 % and 5.0 %).
  • Status of the lumped model: validated against MFC's own fixed-wall response (the twins' linear response reproduces the decay); the comparison with the steady Nusselt number is a quasi-steady estimate whose error is about \(\tau_{bl}/\tau\) (about 1 \(D/U\) over the decay time at Re 20).
  • The default wall under-predicts the heat a cold wall absorbs from hot gas by 21 to 52 % at the earliest time and by 1.4 to 9.6 % at 0.1 \(R^2/\alpha_\infty\), falling with refinement at roughly first order (21 % at 40 cells per R at 0.003 against 52 % at 10). The wall layer is thinner than a cell for most of this transient, and the ghost mirror carries at most the flux of a layer one cell thick.
  • With patch_ib(1)%wall_column the heat is within 1.3 % of the reference from 0.01 \(R^2/\alpha_\infty\) on at 20 and 40 cells per R, and within 2.5 % at 0.03 on all three grids. It over-predicts at the earliest time (+6.0 % at 40 cells per R, +12.7 % at 10), a bias that does not fall with refinement in the range run.
  • Partially validated: the lumped cooling is two Reynolds numbers on two coarse grids against MFC's own fixed-wall Nusselt number; the cold wall has a low-Mach reference only; a moving or reacting wall and a sphere were not run.

Known limitations

  • Low-Mach column. The conduction column is a low-Mach sub-grid model; its early over-prediction (+6 % at 0.003 \(R^2/\alpha_\infty\), 22 ns for a 50 um particle in 1500 K air) is probably the thermoacoustic reduction of the heat of a compressible gas that a constant-pressure reference does not have (the pressure of the gas next to the wall dips while it cools). A compressible reference was not run here, so the size of this effect is not verified. It decays with time and is at or below 1.3 % by 0.03 \(R^2/\alpha_\infty\) (0.22 us), a small part of the gas-side heating time of a particle.
  • The heat booked on the body at the first step of a run differs from what the columns drain by several percent (observed on a reacting wall, not traced); the earliest entries of the cold-wall table may include it.
  • The column applies to a fixed isothermal circle or sphere with chemistry, chem_params%diffusion, chem_params%transport_model = 1 and an adaptive or constant CFL step, and was run on circles here.
  • The lumped model is one temperature per body (small Biot number), explicit in the update and has no recession.
  • The lumped reference is MFC's own fixed-wall Nusselt number: an error common to the fixed and lumped runs (for example the first-order wall) does not appear in it. A lumped body's decay agrees with the steady Nusselt number only to about \(\tau_{bl}/\tau\), with \(\tau_{bl}\) about 1 \(D/U\) at Re 20.

python3 examples/2D_ibm_cold_wall_transient/check_heat.py examples/2D_ibm_cold_wall_transient

Each run writes into the example directory, so copy it between runs. The 40 cells per R cold-wall run takes about 20 minutes on one core. The suite runs the CI-sized cold wall (12 cells per R, 0.03 \(R^2/\alpha_\infty\)) as 2D -> Chemistry -> IBM -> Cold Wall Transient -> Wall Column.
