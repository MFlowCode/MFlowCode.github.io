Validation: Periodic Particle Arrays

Periodic arrays of fixed particles: drag, heat transfer and the reacting-array budget

Status: partially validated. Code: master at 0cdfdf02a. Example: examples/2D_ibm_periodic_array. Last run: 2026-11-23.

Purpose

Particle-resolved training and closure data for gas-solid flow come from periodic boxes holding many particles, driven at a fixed bulk flow, with a force and a heat flux recorded for every particle. This checks that configuration: the global momentum balance of the periodic box, the mean drag of random sphere arrays against the Tenneti, Garg and Subramaniam correlation Tenneti et al. [94], the spread of the per-particle force, and the Nusselt number against Gunn [29]. It also records what is known of the burning-array mass budget.

Setup

Item Value
Geometry N fixed no-slip isothermal bodies in a periodic box, placed by seeded random sequential addition (minimum surface gap 0.1 D, bodies may straddle the periodic faces); 2D cylinders or (--dim 3) spheres
Flow dry air (h2o2.yaml, chemistry on for thermodynamics and mixture-averaged transport, reactions off), 300 K, 1 atm; the fluid-averaged mass flux is held at \(Q = \mathrm{Re}_m \mu/((1-\phi) D)\) by mean_mflux with mean_dpdx_gain = 0.05, so \(\mathrm{Re}_m = \rho(1-\phi) W D/\mu\) with \(W\) the fluid-phase mean velocity; Mach 0.3 (3D), 0.2 (2D)
Grid 16 and 24 cells/D, uniform; 3D: 20 spheres at \(\phi\) = 0.2 in a cube of 3.74 D (0.26 and 0.88 M cells)
Boundaries triply (2D: doubly) periodic
Wall fixed, no slip, thermal_bc = 1 at the free-stream temperature (they absorb the heat the driving force dissipates); mass = 0 keeps a body-force term out of the force record
Numerics WENO5-M, HLLC, SSP-RK3, weno_Re_flux, fd_order = 4, fixed step at acoustic CFL 0.2 (3D) or 0.3 (2D) because the controller needs a constant dt; ib_ip_interp = 1; these runs used ib_wall_order = 1 and ib_visc_compact = F, the defaults then (the Reproduce commands pass --wall_order 1 --visc_compact 0; the example now follows the solver defaults 2 and T)
Length \(t W/D\) = 10 (3D), 43 (2D), averages over the last 40 %
Hardware NVIDIA A100 80GB, NVHPC 25.11, double precision

Reference data

  • Tenneti, Garg and Subramaniam Tenneti et al. [94], Eq. 43: the total force per particle over \(F_\mathrm{St} = 3\pi\mu D(1-\phi)W\), fit range \(0.1 \le \phi \le 0.5\), \(\mathrm{Re}_m \le 300\). The immersed-boundary force has no mean-pressure-gradient part, so the total force is the recorded force over \((1-\phi)\) (their Eqs. 33 and 38). The authors report an average deviation of 2.5 % of the fit from their data.
  • Gunn [29] for the particle Nusselt number of a fixed bed, with \(\epsilon = 1-\phi\) and \(\mathrm{Re} = \mathrm{Re}_m\); and Sun, Tenneti and Subramaniam Sun et al. [88] for the thermally developed state (mean_temp_wall).
  • Exact: the momentum balance of a periodic box, \(\sum_i F_{x,i} = -\partial_x \bar p\, V_f\) at steady state.
  • No reference exists for the 2D cylinder array: its checks are the balance and the spread.

Quantities

All from check_array.py on the run directory (D/ib_forces.dat, D/ib_heat.dat, mean_dpdx.dat; no field output).

  • Q / Q*: the held mass flux over its target.
  • balance: \(\sum_i F_{x,i}/(-\partial_x\bar p\,V_f)\), with \(V_f\) the fluid cells of the staircase mask (the controller's definition).
  • F / F_St (3D), Cd (2D, total force per length over \(\tfrac12\rho((1-\phi)W)^2 D\)).
  • spread: standard deviation over the mean of the time-averaged streamwise force of the bodies.
  • Nu (--nu, runs with --thermostat): the heat the bodies give the gas over \(N \pi D^2 k_f (T_s - T_m)\), \(k_f\) at the film temperature, \(T_m\) = mean_temp.

Results

2D, 12 cylinders at \(\phi\) = 0.2, \(\mathrm{Re}_m\) = 20, 14 cells/D (the CI-sized case run to \(tW/D\) = 43): Q/Q* = 1.000000, balance 1.0000, Cd 9.58, spread 0.44 (bodies between 0.40 and 1.72 of the mean).

Nusselt number

Thermally developed state (--thermostat: mean_temp_wall at the body temperature, mixing-cup temperature held at 300 K), 20 spheres at \(\phi\) = 0.2, \(\mathrm{Re}_m\) = 20, 16 cells/D, Mach 0.1, \(tW/D\) = 8, last 30 %, bodies at 290 K and 310 K (seed 1). Gunn gives 5.995 and the Sun et al. correlation 3.39 on the same (surface-area) basis, from the equation as fitted to their data (a mixing-cup Nusselt number per cross-section perimeter, times \(\pi/4\)).

The thermostat removes the viscous dissipation from the energy equation (mean_temp_wall with viscous, Patankar et al. [67]); the first columns are with it removed (A100, NVHPC 25.11, one rank, last 40 %), the last column is before (last 30 %).

bodies Nu Gunn difference F/F_St Nu before the removal
290 K (cold) 6.113 5.995 +2.0 % 7.361 6.647
310 K (hot) 5.711 5.998 -4.8 % 7.432 5.039
mean of the pair 5.91 5.996 -1.4 % 5.84

The hot and cold runs disagree by 7 % (28 % before the removal, which was mostly viscous heating), so the pair mean is the quotable number: 1.4 % below Gunn and 1.7 times the Sun et al. value. The drag does not depend on the temperature difference (7.36 and 7.43).

Burning array (not repeated on master)

A reacting array (carbon spheres in hot air, the Bradley-Chelliah surface mechanism of examples/2D_ibm_reacting_surface, lumped solid wall thermal_bc = 3, mean_temp thermostat) was run in earlier development builds, not on master, and is not part of the example or the suite (11-species chemistry is too heavy for the suite). The measurements are quoted with their settings: 64 spheres at \(\phi\) = 0.10 in a cube of 6.96 D, 2000 K and 10 atm air, Re_m 100 held by mean_mflux, 24 cells/D, ib_ip_interp = 2, one realization, two flow-throughs.

  • The per-particle gasification rate agreed with a single-film (kinetic plus diffusion) estimate to +2 % at 24 and 32 cells/D and was 8 % low at 12 cells/D.
  • The gas of the closed box gained 9.5 % more mass than the surface chemistry gasified at 24 cells/D, falling with resolution (28, 23, 9.5 and 5.2 % at 12, 16, 24 and 32 cells/D): a resolution error of the ghost-cell wall, which the opt-in ib_flux_wall closes (the face carbon equals the face mass flux to round-off).
  • Drag was +7.6 % above Tenneti and the Nusselt number (conductive, mixing-cup temperature) +6 % above Gunn, within the scatter of the non-reacting arrays above.

The single-body closed-box budget is reproducible on master with examples/2D_ibm_wall_mass_budget.

Assessment

  • The momentum balance closes to 1e-4 in every run and the controller holds the flux exactly: the periodic box, the force record and the controller agree.
  • At \(\mathrm{Re}_m\) = 20 the mean drag of two 20-sphere realizations is 2 % below Tenneti (the realizations differ by 4 %, the size of the scatter between realizations of such small boxes). At \(\mathrm{Re}_m\) = 100 it is 11 to 14 % above at 16 cells/D.
  • Status is partially validated: boxes of 20 spheres, two realizations and at most two grids are not enough to separate the correlation's own 2.5 % deviation, the realization scatter and the grid error.

Known limitations

  • The Re 100 excess is a grid effect in part: it changes little from 16 to 24 cells/D (+10.7 % to +10.2 % for seed 1), so the 20-sphere boxes here do not show the 7 to 8 % grid error of the reference. Tenneti et al. report that their own drag converges from below with resolution and that 20 cells/D is about 7 to 8 % low at \(\phi\) = 0.2 (their Fig. 3, read from the plot), so the correlation itself may sit below a converged solution; this is an argument about the reference, not a measurement of MFC.
  • The boxes hold 20 spheres in a 3.74 D cube; the literature arrays hold 80 or more. Realization scatter is large (4 % at Re 20 here).
  • Not run: 3D arrays above 1 M cells and the 80-particle boxes (0.8 to 5.8 M cells); the pre-merge drag, Mach and per-particle force tables of those runs are not repeated here.
  • Nusselt number, open. The hot and cold bodies of the thermostat pair differ by 7 % (6.11 and 5.71 against 5.995 for Gunn). Against the Sun et al. correlation the pair mean is 1.7 times higher (earlier runs: 1.7 to 1.9), which is not explained (the methods, constant against variable properties and the Mach number differ). Treat the Nusselt numbers as plus or minus 15 %. At Mach 0.3 (Brinkman number 0.77) the hot run used to cool its core to 1 K and abort at \(tW/D\) = 8.3 (#2418); with the dissipation removed it completes \(tW/D\) = 12 with the fluid between 253 K and 322 K. The cell-centre dissipation takes out about 85 % of the pump work at 16 cells/D, so the rest still biases high-Mach runs: use Mach 0.1 for a Nusselt number.
  • A closed periodic box with isothermal bodies gains mass slowly (documented in Accuracy Limits of the Ghost-Cell Immersed Boundary and the mass-budget example examples/2D_ibm_wall_mass_budget).
  • A body force on moving particles is not a mean pressure gradient: with mean_dpdx the force is not applied inside the bodies, so use the controller for fixed or moving arrays alike.
