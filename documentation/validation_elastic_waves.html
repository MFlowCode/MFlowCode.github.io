Validation: Hypoelastic Wave Speeds

Hypoelastic solids: longitudinal and shear Riemann problems

Status: validated (first order in the jump, at small jumps). Code: master at 5838b641c. Examples: examples/1D_sodHypo, examples/2D_hypo_shear_contact. See also Validation.

With hypoelasticity = T the deviatoric stress obeys \(\dot\tau = 2G\,\mathrm{dev}(D)\) plus the objective rotation terms, so a 1D compression changes \(\tau_{xx}\) at the rate \(\frac{4}{3}G\,\partial_x u\) and a shear changes \(\tau_{xy}\) at \(G\,\partial_x v\). Linear elastodynamics then gives longitudinal waves at \(c_L = \sqrt{(K + 4G/3)/\rho}\), with \(K = \gamma(p + \pi_\infty)\) the bulk modulus of the stiffened gas, and shear waves at \(c_S = \sqrt{G/\rho}\). For a jump at rest, the Riemann solution of the linear system is

  • normal stress \(\sigma = -p + \tau_{xx}\): \(u^* = (\sigma_R - \sigma_L)/(Z_l + Z_r)\) with \(Z = \rho c_L\); across each longitudinal wave \(dp = -(K/M)\,d\sigma\) and \(d\tau_{xx} = (4G/3)/M\,d\sigma\), \(M = K + 4G/3\);
  • shear stress: \(v^* - v_0 = (\tau_{xy,R} - \tau_{xy,L})/(Z_l + Z_r)\) with \(Z = \rho c_S\), and \(\tau_{xy}^* = \tau_{xy,L} + Z_l (v^* - v_0)\).

examples/1D_sodHypo/check_elastic.py measures the plateau values (median over the middle 60 % of each plateau) and the front positions (where u, or \(v - v_0\), crosses half its plateau value) and prints their errors against these formulas. All tables were measured with GNU 13.3 (CPU, one rank, double precision) on master at 5838b641c; one resolution of each was repeated on an NVIDIA A100 (NVHPC 25.11, OpenACC) with the same digits shown, except where noted.

The front speeds converge to \(c_L\) (to 4e-5 at 1616 cells); the plateau stresses level off at 2.4e-5 of the jump, which is the size of the neglected second-order terms ( \(\Delta\sigma/M\) = 6e-5). The 404-cell run on the A100 gives the same values to the digits printed.

Example jump (1e8 Pa, \(\Delta\sigma/M\) = 6e-3), 202 cells: \(u^*\) +6.1e-5, worst plateau stress +2.8e-3, front speeds −1.4e-3 and +2.3e-3. The errors are O( \(\Delta\sigma/M\)), as expected of a first-order prediction for a jump this size; the speed of the compressive (right-moving) wave is above \(c_L\) and that of the expansion below it.

The plateau is exact to round-off once the waves have left the contact region (at 64 cells the plateau is only a few cells wide). The front speeds converge at first order, the order of the scheme: both measured fronts sit about 0.2 cells to the left of the linear positions at every resolution, which makes the left wave look fast and the right one slow by the same amount. The 128-cell run on the A100 gives the same values.

Limits: linear waves only (the jumps are small or their nonlinear error is reported); one material per test; no plastic or damage models; the hyperelastic model (hyperelasticity) is not covered.
